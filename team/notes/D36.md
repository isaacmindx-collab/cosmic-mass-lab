## Pre-registration (committed before any code of this task was written and before any run)

No exploratory run was made. The numbers below are arithmetic from the formulas. Every case runs under the reference law **L0**, so every statement will be **mathematics**: a check of a numerical method, not a finding about nature.

**The three candidates of question 12**
- **A, nearest image (D36, to be built in the engine):** each pair of bodies interacts once, through the closest of its periodic copies; positions are wrapped back into the box.
- **B, open-space forces:** positions wrap, forces ignore the copies. Written in the scenario script only.
- **C, the full periodic sum:** every copy acts. Written in the scenario script only, as a plain sum over the copies in the square shells `|i|, |j| <= n`, for a pair with no net charge (for which the sum converges), at `n` = 50, 100, 200.

**Settings common to all:** scaled units (`G_N = K = 1`, `s = 1`), 2D (motion in a plane, `1/r^2` field: D21), `phi_bar` = 90 degrees, a square box of side `L = 1` with corners (0, 0) and (1, 1).

### D36.1: the force on one body of a pair (forces evaluated once)
- **Setup:** two bodies with charges +1 and -1 and masses 1e-12 (so that Newton's part of the force is 1e-24 of Coulomb's), on the line y = 0.5.
  - (a) straddling the edge: at x = 0.02 and x = 0.98. Their true separation through the edge is 0.04.
  - (b) inside the box, centred, at separations `d` = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4.
- **Predictions:**
  - (a) A gives the attraction `1 / 0.04^2 = 625` to 1e-12. B sees the separation 0.96 and gives `1 / 0.96^2 = 1.085`, 1.7e-3 of the right force.
  - (b) A and B are identical (the pair does not straddle the edge). Against C: the copies of the pair are a square lattice of dipoles `p = d`. To leading order they make a uniform field `p S / (2 L^3)` along the dipole at the pair, with `S = sum 1/R^3` over the square lattice `= 4 zeta(3/2) beta(3/2) = 9.0336`. It pushes the positive charge away from the negative one, so **A overestimates the attraction by the fraction `4.517 (d/L)^3`**: 5.6e-4, 4.5e-3, 3.6e-2 and 0.29 at the four separations. The next term is of relative order `(d/L)^2` of this one.
  - C has converged: the value at `n` = 200 differs from `n` = 100 by less than 1e-3 of the correction itself.
- **Pass:** (a) as stated. (b) `(F_A - F_C) / F_A` is positive and within 20% of `4.517 (d/L)^3` at `d` = 0.05 and 0.1, and within a factor 2 at 0.2. At `d` = 0.4 it is reported, not judged (the leading term is not enough there).

### D36.2: Newton's third law
- **Setup:** the gas of D36.4 at its initial state.
- **Prediction:** A and B are both pairwise, so under both the sum of all forces is zero to rounding: below 1e-12 of the sum of the sizes of the forces. The third law does **not** tell A from B. (C would pass too; it is not computed for 40 bodies.)
- **Pass:** both residuals below 1e-12.

### D36.3: a bound pair carried across the edge
- **Setup:** two neutral bodies of mass 1 on a circular orbit of separation `a = 0.02` (relative speed `sqrt(2 / a) = 10`, period `2 pi a / 10 = 0.012566`), centred at (0.5, 0.5), with a common velocity (5, 0) added. Softening 0, the adaptive step of D29 with `eta = 1e-3`, `t_end = 0.35`. The centre of mass crosses the edge at t = 0.1 and t = 0.3; each time the pair straddles the edge for a third of a period. Reference: the same pair in open space (no box), same common velocity.
- **Predictions:**
  - A: the pair does not notice the edge. The separation stays `a` within 1e-5 throughout; the energy (computed with the nearest image) has end drift and largest excursion below 1e-6; the final relative position agrees with the open-space run to 1e-6 of `a`; the step count is within 1% of the open-space run.
  - B: at the first crossing the two bodies see each other at a distance of about `L` and fly apart: the separation at the end, measured through the nearest image, exceeds `10 a`.
- **Pass:** all of the above.

### D36.4: a gas in a periodic box
- **Setup:** 40 bodies of mass 1, 20 with charge +0.05 and 20 with -0.05, places uniform in the box and velocities normal with spread 0.3 on each axis, drawn with seed 11; softening 0.02; adaptive step, `eta = 1e-3`; 4000 steps. Run under A by the engine's runner (the stamped run), and under A and B by the script's own loop (to count the crossings and to give B the same treatment).
- **Predictions:**
  - A: mass and charge exactly constant; every body inside the box at the end; momentum drift below 1e-12 (the forces are pairwise); at least 20 edge crossings in the run (speed 0.3 times the elapsed time, of order 0.5, in a box of side 1). Energy with the nearest image: end drift below 1e-3. This number is the least certain: the nearest-image force of an unscreened `1/r^2` field jumps when a pair's closest copy changes (at a separation of half the box along an axis), and velocity Verlet does not like jumps. This is the "stated limit" of D36, and the run measures its size.
  - B: the energy (open-space formula) jumps at each crossing; its end drift is at least 10 times that of A.
  - The loop of the script and the engine's runner agree for A: same final positions to 1e-9.
- **Pass:** all of the above. A failed prediction is reported as such.

**Analysis plan:** the quantities above, each with its flag, in one `summary.json` per test (`results/D36.1` to `results/D36.4`), written by `scenarios/D36/run.py`. End drift and largest excursion are both reported (D21.3).

**What a pass would and would not mean:** it would show that the engine's nearest image does what D36 says and that B is not a usable alternative, and it would put a number on A's error against the full sum for one simple case. It would not show that the nearest image is adequate for a given physical study: that depends on the box being much larger than the structures studied, which each scenario must argue for itself.
