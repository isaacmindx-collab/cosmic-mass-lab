## Part A (this pull request): L1b, L1c, L1d and L7

### Definitions (labelled choices)
1. **One rule for every reading:** force = L0's `Re[mu (E_cal + v x B_cal)]` plus `+/- lambda` (L2, L3) times what the reading adds to it. For L1a this is `docs/05`'s formula (`Re + lambda s Com`). It is the only rule under which the reduce-to-L0 test of the work plan can hold for every law: read literally, `docs/05`'s "L1c: force = Ht(mu E_cal)" drops the Real part, and a pure mass would then feel **no gravity at all**.
2. With `C` the selected Composer terms of `mu E_cal` (option L5) and `R = Re(mu E_cal)`, what each reading adds:
   - **L1a:** `s C` (unchanged);
   - **L1b:** `s cos(phi_bar) C`, which is `Sh - Re`;
   - **L1c:** `s sin(phi_bar) C`, which is `Ht`;
   - **L1d:** `|A| u - R`, where `Sh = R + s cos(phi_bar) C` and `Ht = s sin(phi_bar) C` are vectors, `|A| = sqrt(|Sh|^2 + |Ht|^2)` is the length of the vector `mu E_cal`, and `u` is the unit vector of `Sh` (of `C` if `Sh` is zero). At lambda = 1 the force is `|A| u`: "abs along the field". **Lengths of vectors, not of components**, so that the force does not depend on the orientation of the axes. For a body whose Composer coordinate is zero (a neutral mass in gravity) L1d adds nothing.
3. **L7 (E3.4):** `Re(mu) = m`; `Sh(mu) = m + q s cos(phi_bar)`; `abs(mu) = sqrt(Sh^2 + Ht^2)`. `Sh(mu)` can be negative (an electron in SI: -3.3e-10 kg); kept as the hypothesis says (T8 explores it). A zero inertia is refused by the integrator (existing). L0 takes no option, as before.
