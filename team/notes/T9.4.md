## Goal
Show that the engine reproduces the book's statement (Chapter 9, "particle against antiparticle", and the table of tests): set beside the same neutral mass, a particle and its antiparticle differ only in the Coupling term, so if the Composer coordinate were read as a force they would be pushed in opposite directions. Setup and pass criterion: `docs/05-scenarios-and-tests.md`, section 2, T9.4: L1a, lambda = 1; neutral Earth; a proton, then an antiproton (`m_p - e G`), at 7e6 m; "opposite pushes".

## Pre-registration (commit before any production run)
Committed before any code of this test was written or run. No exploratory run was made. The numbers below come from the formulas only (a few lines of arithmetic with the constants of `engine/cosmicmass/constants.py`), not from the engine.

- **Question:** under the hypothesis L1a (the Composer coordinate of `mu E_cal` read as a second force along the field, sign +, strength lambda = 1, all terms), does the engine give a proton and an antiproton at the same place near the neutral Earth Composer forces of the same size and opposite direction, with the same Real force?
- **Law(s):** L1a, a **hypothesis** (D1), as implemented in `laws/l1.py`: force = `Re(mu E_cal) + lambda s Com(mu E_cal)`, with `Com(mu E_cal) = q g + m e + 2 s cos(phi_bar) q e`. All terms, lambda = 1, sign +.
- **Matter:** the antiparticle of `mu = m + q G` is `m - q G`, the skew flip (book, Chapter 10, "The Antiparticle on the Frame"). Antiproton: `m = 1.67262192e-27` kg (the proton preset), `q = -1.602176634e-19` C. It is not a preset; it is given in the settings by `m` and `q`.
- **Settings (as in stamp.json):** SI, 2D, `phi_bar` = 79.86 deg, softening 0 (D21), no time integration: the forces are evaluated once at the starting positions. Earth (preset, `q = 0`) fixed at the origin; the test body at (7e6 m, 0), at rest. Two cases: `proton`, `antiproton`.
- **Prediction and why (analytic):**
  - The Earth has `q = 0`, so its field at the body has `e = 0`: the terms `m e` and `2 s cos(phi_bar) q e` vanish, and only `s q g` is left. This holds at every frame angle.
  - `g = G_N M / r^2` = 8.13447 m/s^2, towards the Earth.
  - Composer force: `s q g`. Proton (`q = +e`): **1.51237e-8 N towards the Earth** (an extra pull). Antiproton (`q = -e`): **1.51237e-8 N away from the Earth** (a push). Their sum is zero.
  - Real force: `m g` = 1.36059e-26 N towards the Earth, **the same** for both (the masses are equal): under L0 they fall alike.
  - Size of the Composer force against the weight: `s e / m_p` = **1.1116e18**. With the largest strength that T9.1 allows (lambda = 0.0020), it would still be 2.2e15 times the weight (labelled mathematics; no measurement of the fall of a charged antiparticle exists, as the book states).
- **Analysis plan (quantities, how pass / fail / finding is judged):**
  - From the engine (field solver `DirectSum` and the law `L1a`), for each case: the Composer force, the Real force, and the three terms of `Com(mu E_cal)` separately (by running L1a with each `terms` option).
  - **Pass** if all hold:
    1. the Composer force on the antiproton equals minus the one on the proton, to 1e-15 relative ("opposite pushes");
    2. the proton's Composer force points towards the Earth and the antiproton's away from it;
    3. each has the size `s e g` to 1e-12 relative;
    4. the Real forces of the two cases are equal (bit for bit: same mass, same field);
    5. the terms `-m E/s` and `-2 cos q E` are exactly zero (the Earth is neutral).
  - Also reported (not part of the verdict): the ratio of the Composer force to the weight; the same at lambda = 0.0020; the result at `phi_bar` = 120 deg (expected identical); and lambda = 0 gives L0 (Composer force zero).
  - Kind: `measurement-check`, as for T9.1 and T9.3 (same gate, D4). It reproduces a statement of the book. There is **no measurement** to compare with: the book says the fall of a charged antiparticle has not been measured, and antihydrogen is neutral and tests nothing here. The summary says so.
