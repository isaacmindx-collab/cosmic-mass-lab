## Goal
The session that built the web universe found that scenes moving as a whole (Sun, Earth and Moon) take millions of steps, because the adaptive step uses the absolute speed of each body (`team/notes/UI1.md`), and suggested a scenario. The rule in question is mine (MVP-6) and is part of decision D21, convention 4. This scenario measures the problem and tests one alternative. It changes no default.

## Pre-registration (commit before any production run)
Committed before any code of this scenario was written or run. No exploratory run was made. What I knew beforehand: the remark of `team/notes/UI1.md`, and the results of T0.1 (6,283 steps and a period error of 3.3e-7 for the Earth and moon in their centre-of-mass frame).

- **Question:** the laws of motion do not depend on a uniform velocity added to every body. Does the engine's step? And does a rule based on each body's motion relative to its nearest neighbour remove the dependence without losing accuracy?
- **Law(s):** L0 only. This is a question about the numerical method.
- **Candidates:**
  - **A, the current rule (D10 and D21, convention 4):** for each body, `|a| dt <= eta |v|` with the floor `0.1 sqrt(|a| l)` on the speed, and `|v| dt <= eta l`, where `v` and `a` are the body's own velocity and acceleration and `l` the distance to its nearest neighbour.
  - **B, pair-relative:** the same three conditions, with `v` and `a` replaced by the velocity and acceleration of the body **relative to its nearest neighbour**. Unchanged for a body with no neighbour (no limit).
- **Settings:** SI, 2D, 79.86 deg, `eta` = 1e-3, softening 0.
  1. Earth and moon in their centre-of-mass frame, one period (the setup of T0.1a).
  2. The same with 29,780 m/s added to both bodies along x (the Earth's speed around the Sun).
  3. Two protons at 1e-10 m, at rest, then with 1e6 m/s added to both (the setup of T0.1c).
  4. Sun, Earth and moon: Sun at rest at the origin, Earth at 1.496e11 m with 29,780 m/s, moon 3.844e8 m further out with 1,022 m/s more; total momentum not removed; 27.3 days.
- **Predictions:**
  - 1: A as in T0.1a (6,283 steps, period error 3.3e-7). B: the relative speed and acceleration of a two-body circular orbit give `eta r / v_rel`, the same step as A, so the same steps and error to a few parts in a thousand.
  - 2: A: the displacement limit `eta l / |v|` = 1e-3 x 3.844e8 / 29,780 = 12.9 s governs, so about 2,357,430 / 12.9 = **183,000 steps, 29 times more** than in case 1, with a smaller period error. B: the same number of steps and the same error as in case 1, to rounding (the boost cancels in every relative quantity).
  - 3: A boosted: the step is `eta l / 1e6`, about 1e-19 s at the start, so tens of thousands of steps instead of 3,245; B: the same steps and separation error in both frames (3.8e-7).
  - 4: A: governed by the moon's absolute speed, about 180,000 steps. B: about 6,300 to 13,000 steps (the moon's acceleration relative to the Earth includes the Sun's tide, a little larger than in case 1). Accuracy is judged against a reference run of B with `eta` = 1.25e-4: the Earth-moon separation at the end should agree to 1e-5 for A and for B.
- **Analysis plan:** number of steps; period error against the analytic period (1, 2); separation error against the closed form (3); in case 4 the Earth-moon separation at the end against the reference run, and the energy (end drift and largest excursion, D21). For B, the largest difference between the boosted and unboosted relative trajectories.
- **What a result would and would not mean:** if B is as accurate as A at a fraction of the steps and independent of the boost, it is a candidate to replace convention 4 of D21. **It would not be enough to adopt it.** B looks only at the nearest neighbour. A close pair that travels fast through the changing field of a third body has small relative speed and acceleration, and B could then take steps too long for the third body's field. That case is not tested here; it is listed under "Open questions" and must be studied before any change of D21.

| Setup | Rule A (current: absolute) | Rule B (relative to the nearest neighbour) |
|---|---|---|

| 2. The same, plus 29,780 m/s | **184,660 steps**; period error 3.9e-10 | 6,284 steps; period error 3.3e-7; final separation equal to setup 1 within 1.4e-12 |

(Reference of setup 4: rule B with `eta` = 1.25e-4, 51,588 steps. Energy end drift and largest excursion are below 3.3e-7 in every run and are in `summary.json`.)

What the runs show:
- **The current rule depends on the frame.** Adding a uniform velocity to all bodies, which changes nothing physical, multiplies the number of steps by 29 for the Earth and moon and by 10.5 for the two protons. It does not harm accuracy; the extra steps make the result more accurate than asked. It is a cost, not an error. In a scene that moves as a whole, such as the Sun, Earth and moon, it is the whole cost: 186,692 steps where 6,449 give the answer to 2.8e-8.
- **Rule B does not depend on the frame:** the same steps and the same errors with and without the boost, and the same final separation to 1.4e-12.
- **Rule B is as accurate as rule A is in the pair's own frame**, on these four setups.

Compared with the predictions (8 flags in `summary.json`, 7 hold):
- Setup 1, 2 and 4: as predicted (29 times more steps for A when boosted; B unchanged; A about 180,000 and B about 6,400 steps in setup 4; both within 1e-5 of the reference).
- **Setup 3, one flag fails and one prediction was wrong in its value.** I wrote that A boosted would take "tens of thousands of steps instead of 3,245": it takes 34,167, which is that. But the flag in my script asked for more than ten times the steps of **B** in its own frame (3,961), and 34,167 is 8.6 times that, so the flag fails. And I predicted that B would reproduce A's own-frame numbers for the protons (3,245 steps, 3.8e-7): it does not. For two bodies released at rest the relative acceleration is twice each body's own, so B's floor of the speed gives a different step: 3,961 steps and a smaller error, 1.3e-7. What I did predict correctly is that B is the same in both frames.

## Open questions
- **Rule B is not proposed as the new default yet.** It looks only at the nearest neighbour. A close pair that travels fast through the changing field of a third body has a small relative speed and acceleration, and B could take steps that are too long for the third body's field. Setup 4 does not test this (the Sun's field changes slowly over a month). Before any change of decision D21, convention 4, this case needs its own scenario: for example a tight pair on a fast, close passage by a heavy body, with B, and with B plus a guard (such as the D10 condition taken in the frame of the centre of mass of all bodies). Recorded as question 14 of `team/QUESTIONS.md`.
- Until then the option exists (`numerics.speed_reference = "neighbour"`) and a scenario or the web universe may choose it, saying so in its stamp.
