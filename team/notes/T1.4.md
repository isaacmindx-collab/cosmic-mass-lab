## Pre-registration (commit before any production run)
**This run is exploratory by decision of the MVP plan** (row MVP-10), so its stamps say `"preregistered": false`. I still wrote down, before running anything, what I expect (this file is committed first):

- **Question:** started on the circular orbit of L0, what does the pair do under L1a with lambda = 1?
- **Law(s):** L0 (reference); L1a (hypothesis), sign +, lambda = 1, all terms.
- **Settings:** SI, 2D, `phi_bar` = 79.86 deg; proton and electron presets at 5.29177e-11 m with the circular speed of L0; softening 0; adaptive step, `eta` = 1e-3; both runs last 5 periods of the L0 orbit.
- **Expectation and why:** under L1a the electron feels, besides Coulomb's pull `k / r^2`, the charge share `-2 cos(phi_bar) q E`, which pushes back with `2 cos(79.86 deg)` = 0.352 of that pull (the book, Chapter 9, gives the same 0.352), and the Coupling term, which is 9e-19 of it and invisible here. The pull is therefore `f k / r^2` with `f` = 0.648. A Kepler orbit with that pull, started at the L0 circular speed, is an ellipse with its nearest point at the start: energy `(1/2 - f) k / r0`, semi-major axis `a = f / (2f - 1) r0` = 2.191 r0, eccentricity `1 - r0 / a` = 0.544, farthest separation 3.38 r0, period `a^1.5 / sqrt(f)` = 4.03 L0 periods. So in 5 L0 periods the pair should go out to 3.38 r0 once and come back: **bound, no spiral, no precession** (the force stays `1/r^2`).
- **Why it is only a demo:** L1a with lambda = 1 is excluded by measurement (T9.1: lambda below 0.0020; the book's hydrogen argument: 5.71 eV instead of 13.598 eV). The demo shows what the engine does with a reading, not what nature does.
- **What is reported:** smallest and largest separation, the time of the first return to the smallest separation, the drift of the L0 energy and of the momentum (under L1a neither is a conserved quantity of the law: the Coupling term does not obey Newton's third law). *(Written before the run; the remark on the third law was wrong, see the correction under Results.)*

## Results
**Exploratory. Not a result; not to be cited (D12).** Run ids: `results/T1.4/L0/20261110-185903-30f2172`, `results/T1.4/L1a/20261110-185905-30f2172` (stamps: `"preregistered": false`). Figures: `figures/orbit.svg` and `figures/separation.svg` in the L1a folder.

What the virtual universe did (**finding of the virtual universe, exploratory**):
- **Under L0** the pair stays on its circle: separation constant to four digits over 5 periods (31,416 steps), L0 energy drift 8.5e-15.
- **Under L1a, lambda = 1** (hypothesis) the same start gives a bound, closed, eccentric orbit: the separation goes from 1.0000 r0 to **3.3808 r0** and is back at 1.0000 r0 after **4.03** L0 periods (4.026 at the sampling of the time series). No spiral and no visible precession over the 1.24 turns covered.
- **Compared with the expectation written before running:** farthest separation 3.3808 r0 expected, 3.3808 r0 found; return after 4.03 L0 periods expected, 4.03 found. So in this pair L1a at lambda = 1 acts as Coulomb's pull reduced to 0.648 of itself by the charge share, as the book's Chapter 9 describes.
- The L0 energy changes by 44% of its initial value during the L1a run. This is not an error of the integrator: the L0 energy is not a conserved quantity of L1a. The engine has no energy for the Composer coordinate yet (it needs an approved derivation), so the conservation of L1a cannot be judged from this run.
- The momentum drift under L1a is 3.3e-15, the same size as under L0. **Corrected on 2026-11-10 (scenario Q1, `team/notes/Q.md`):** an earlier version of this line said that the Coupling term does not obey Newton's third law. That holds only for each half of it taken alone; with all terms, as in this demo, L1a obeys the third law exactly, which is why the momentum is conserved to rounding.

What this does **not** say: anything about nature. L1a at lambda = 1 is excluded by the measured charge of the neutron (T9.1, lambda below 0.0020), and a hydrogen atom held with 0.648 of Coulomb's pull would have 5.71 eV of binding instead of the measured 13.598 eV (the book; test T9.2, not run here).
