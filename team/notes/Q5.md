# Q5: does the nearest-neighbour step (rule B of Q4) fail on a fast passage, and does a pair guard fix it?

## Goal
Q4 showed that rule A (the step of D10 and D21, convention 4) depends on the frame, and that rule B (the same conditions, relative to each body's nearest neighbour) does not, at the same accuracy, on four setups. Q4 also named the weakness of B that it did not test: a fast encounter between bodies that are not each other's nearest neighbours is seen by no body's step. Q5 builds that case on purpose, measures B on it, and tests one guard.

## Pre-registration (commit before any production run)
Committed before any code of this scenario was written or run. No exploratory run was made. The estimates below come from a few lines of arithmetic, not from the engine.

- **Question:** (i) on a fast passage of a light, loose pair by a heavy body whose nearest neighbour is its own companion, is rule B less accurate than its `eta` promises? (ii) Does rule C = B plus a pair guard, `dt <= eta min_(i<j) r_ij / |v_i - v_j|` over all pairs of alive bodies, give the accurate answer at a moderate cost? (iii) On the setups of Q4, does C keep B's frame independence and cost?
- **Law(s):** L0 only. A question about the numerical method; every statement is mathematics.
- **Rules:**
  - **A:** the engine's default (`speed_reference = "absolute"`).
  - **B:** `speed_reference = "neighbour"` (Q4).
  - **C:** B, then `dt = min(dt_B, eta min_(i<j) r_ij / |v_i - v_j|)`. The guard uses only relative positions and velocities, so it does not depend on the frame. It is written in the scenario script, **not** in the engine (`integrators.py` belongs to the claimed task E3; no default changes).
  - The time loop is the engine's: `VelocityVerlet.step` with a `StepContext`; the step `dt` comes from the rule; the last step ends on `t_end`.
- **Settings:**
  1. **The trap**, scaled units (`G_N = K = 1`), 2D, frame angle 90 deg (scaled default), softening 0, `eta` = 1e-3, all charges 0:
     - heavy body H: `m` = 1, at the origin, at rest;
     - its companion: `m` = 1e-3, at (1, 0), velocity (0, 1) (a circle of radius 1 around H, period about 2 pi), so H's nearest neighbour is the companion (distance 1) at every moment of the passage;
     - a loose pair: two bodies of `m` = 1e-12 at (-60, 1.5) and (-60, 1.51), both with velocity (300, 0): they pass H at about b = 1.5 (> 1) at V = 300, 37 times the circular speed there, and each one's nearest neighbour is its partner (distance 0.01);
     - `t_end` = 0.4 (the pair goes from x = -60 to x = +60).
  2. **Earth and moon** (Q4 setup 1, SI, 79.86 deg, one period), in its own frame and with 29,780 m/s added to both bodies.
  3. **Sun, Earth and moon** (Q4 setup 4, SI, 27.3 days).
- **Predictions (with the arithmetic):**
  - **Trap, rule B.** H's step is set by its companion: `eta c / v_c` = 1e-3. Each pair member's step is set by its partner: their relative speed is near 0, but their relative acceleration (the tide of H, about `2 G M a / r^3`, 5.9e-3 at r = 1.5) meets the floor of the speed, giving `0.1 eta sqrt(a_pair / tide)` = 1.3e-4 at closest approach. The passage lasts `b / V` = 5e-3, so B takes about 40 steps across it, where `eta` asks for `dt V / r` = 1e-3 per step, and B has about 0.026. **Predicted: B's error on the deflection of the pair** (the change of its centre-of-mass velocity across H's line, `Delta v_y`) **is between 1e-4 and 1e-2 relative** (velocity Verlet: about `(0.026)^2` = 7e-4), far above the 1e-5 that C reaches (below). **Steps: a few hundred to about two thousand.**
  - **Trap, rule C.** The guard gives `dt = eta r / V` at distance r from H, so `dt V / r = eta` throughout: about `(2 / eta) asinh(L / b)` = **8,800 steps** (between 4,000 and 20,000), and a deflection **within 1e-5** of the reference.
  - **Trap, rule A.** Not run: each pair member moves at 300 and its nearest neighbour is 0.01 away, so `dt <= eta l / |v|` = 3.3e-8, which is **about 1.2e7 steps**. Recorded as computed from the rule, not run.
  - **Trap, reference:** rule C with `eta / 8`. Sanity check: the deflection agrees with the straight-line impulse `Delta v_y = 2 G M / (b V) x L / sqrt(L^2 + b^2)` = 4.443e-3 **within 1%** (the companion, of mass 1e-3, and the bending of the path change it by about a part in a thousand).
  - **Earth and moon, rule C:** the guard is `eta r / v_rel` = 1e-3 x 3.844e8 / 1,022 = 376 s, about B's own step (Q4: 6,284 steps over 2.357e6 s, 375 s). **Steps within 10% of B's 6,284, and identical in both frames;** period error as B's (3.3e-7), within a factor 2.
  - **Sun, Earth and moon, rule C:** the Sun-Earth guard is 5,000 s (inactive); the Earth-moon guard as above. **Steps within 10% of B's (Q4: 6,449); Earth-moon separation at the end within 1e-5 of the reference** (rule B with `eta` = 1.25e-4, as in Q4; recomputed here).
- **Analysis plan:** for each run, the number of steps; for the trap, `Delta v_y` of the pair's centre of mass and the final separation of the pair, each against the reference; the energy (end drift and largest excursion, D21) of the trap runs. Convergence of the reference: C at `eta/2`, `eta/4` and `eta/8`, the change of `Delta v_y` from `eta/4` to `eta/8` below 1e-6 relative. Every prediction above is a flag in `summary.json`; the verdict is `finding` (a result about the method, with no external reference).
- **What a result would and would not mean:** if B fails here and C does not, the trap is real, and C is a candidate for convention 4 of D21. It would not prove that C is safe in every case (for example, many bodies passing at once), nor fix its cost in crowded scenes (the guard is `O(N^2)`, as the direct sum already is). Adopting it is a decision for the numerics lead and the maintainer.
